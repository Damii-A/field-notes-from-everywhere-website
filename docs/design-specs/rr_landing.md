# The Reading RoomLanding Page Experience Spec
Working source of truth • September 2026
## Purpose
This document defines the experience of the public-facing Reading Room landing page. It is being developed in two stages so that the page's structure and content are established before its visual identity and design are defined.
### Stage 1 — Page Structure & Content
First, define the actual top-to-bottom structure of the page: what sections exist, what information and content each contains, the order in which The Reading Room is explained, and the actions available to the visitor.
### Stage 2 — Visual Identity & Design
Once the page structure and content are established, define the visual and motion direction for the page: how The Reading Room should feel, how the sections should be presented, and the design principles that should shape the final experience.
Important: The structure-and-content stage should not be interpreted as prescribing the final visual design unless a particular placement, alignment, order or relationship is explicitly stated as a requirement.

## Stage 2 — Visual Identity & Design
### Overall Visual Direction
The Reading Room should feel like a distinct product within the Field Notes From Everywhere world, rather than simply another page of the Publication. It should retain FNFE's warmth, personality and playfulness, but have an identity strong enough that entering The Reading Room feels like entering a different part of the wider brand.
The name The Reading Room can inform the atmosphere of the page, but it should not be interpreted literally. The experience does not need to resemble a physical library, study or reading room, and should avoid obvious visual clichés such as bookshelves, leather chairs, lamps, paper textures or faux-library interiors.
Instead, the page should capture the more appealing idea behind the name: a place made for people who really like books and could happily spend time surrounded by things to read. It should feel inviting, abundant and enjoyable to explore.
There should be a sense of books being everywhere. Book covers, catalogue imagery and glimpses of the product can become important visual material throughout the page. The abundance should feel exciting rather than cluttered. The visitor should come away with the impression that joining The Reading Room opens the door to a substantial and continually growing world of recommendations.
The page can be more playful and expressive than the Publication article experience. This is a product landing page rather than a long-form reading surface, so movement, scale changes, unusual composition and more expressive interactions are welcome where they make the experience more memorable. At the same time, the page should not become so visually busy that the actual offer becomes difficult to understand.
The product itself should remain the star. Screenshots of the Reading Room email, Past Issues and Books views are not incidental illustrations; they are evidence of what the visitor is being offered. The surrounding design should make those product views feel desirable and substantial rather than burying them inside conventional SaaS-style mockups.
### Relationship to the Page Structure
The Section 1 → Section 5 order and content flow established in Stage 1 are fixed. The designer has freedom over the specific layout, composition and transitions used to present those sections, provided the content hierarchy and intended progression are preserved.
The page should feel like one continuous experience, rather than five independent content blocks stacked on top of one another. Transitions, recurring visual ideas, movement or other devices can be used to create continuity as the visitor moves from the daily catalogues, into Past Issues, into Books and finally to the trial offer.
### Motion & Interaction
Motion should contribute to the feeling that The Reading Room is alive, growing and full of books to explore. It can be more noticeable than the restrained motion used inside Publication articles, particularly during transitions between sections and around product imagery.
Potential directions include book covers entering or moving through the composition, collections accumulating as the visitor progresses, product screenshots revealing more of themselves through scroll, or visual elements carrying from one section into the next.
These are examples rather than required animation treatments. Claude Design should be free to find a stronger solution.
Motion should still have purpose. Avoid constant movement simply to make the page feel animated, and provide an appropriate reduced-motion experience.
### Avoid
a conventional SaaS pricing/product page;
a generic newsletter signup landing page;
a literal recreation of a library or reading room;
an e-commerce bookstore;
a database or book-management application;
five visually disconnected promotional sections.
The overall impression should be bookish, playful, abundant and distinctly FNFE, while making The Reading Room itself feel like a product worth entering and spending time with.

# FNFE About Page Experience Spec
Purpose: Define the content flow, page structure, methodology treatment, and design requirements for the public Field Notes From Everywhere About page. This page should introduce FNFE, explain what it produces, provide the canonical public explanation of its recommendation methodology, establish editorial independence, introduce the founder, and provide a contact route.
## 1. Page Role
The About page is a substantive public FNFE page, not a generic corporate profile.
It should help a reader understand what FNFE is, what it does with reader recommendations, how its recommendation research works, why that process is trustworthy, who is behind FNFE, and how to get in touch.
The page inherits the standard public FNFE header and global footer and should clearly belong to the same public-site design system.
The current page copy is provisional/placeholder content. Claude Design should use it to understand hierarchy, flow, approximate content density, and design needs, but should not treat exact wording or paragraph lengths as permanently fixed.
## 2. Required Page Flow
The page should read as one continuous story. The following order is intentional. Claude Design may make visual decisions within each section, but should not rearrange the sections or turn the page into a grid of unrelated modules.
Section 1 — About Field Notes From EverywhereOpen by explaining what FNFE is and that real reader conversations power the publication.
Section 2 — So, what do we do with all those recommendations?Move naturally from the source of the recommendations into what FNFE creates: the Publication and The Reading Room.
Section 3 — How we find the booksThe preceding product explanation should lead into the question of why the recommendations are worth paying attention to. This is the canonical methodology section and must receive special visual and linking treatment.
Section 4 — Our recommendations aren't for saleFollow methodology with the editorial-independence principle: commercial relationships do not determine which books make FNFE recommendation lists.
Section 5 — Meet the FounderAfter the reader understands the publication and its standards, introduce the person behind FNFE in a personal, non-corporate way.
Section 6 — Want to get in touch?Close with a simple contact invitation and general FNFE email address.
## 3. Methodology Callout — Critical Requirement
The entire "How we find the books" section must be visually differentiated from the surrounding About-page content as a substantial methodology callout/container.
It must still feel like part of the natural About-page flow; it should not look like a separate webpage embedded inside the About page.
The methodology section must have its own stable, directly linkable page anchor/URL target.
The "How we made this list" methodology link used on FNFE Publication articles should be able to send a reader directly to this section rather than to the top of the About page.
When a reader arrives through that direct link, the heading "How we find the books" and the methodology context should be immediately apparent.
This is not a small callout card. It needs enough visual and physical space for a substantial explanation of the FNFE research process.
Claude Design may determine the specific visual treatment — for example background, border, typography, spacing, or other design devices — provided the section is clearly distinguished and remains consistent with the FNFE design system.
On mobile, the methodology treatment should remain clearly differentiated and readable, naturally becoming a full-width part of the page where appropriate.
The methodology copy is CMS-managed/provisional and may change in length, so the design must accommodate reasonable content expansion or contraction without depending on a fixed box height.
## 4. Provisional On-Page Copy
Important: The following copy is the current working content and should be used as the design placeholder. It establishes the intended section hierarchy and approximate content density. It will be editable through the CMS and may be revised later.
### 4.1 About Field Notes From Everywhere
We spend a lot of time in spaces where readers gather to talk about books. And those conversations are what power everything we do here at Field Notes From Everywhere.
We're a book discovery publication that takes the recommendations scattered across real reader discussions and turns them into useful, carefully researched collections of books.
Because readers can be remarkably specific about what they want to read and we believe book recommendations should be able to keep up.
### 4.2 So, what do we do with all those recommendations?
We turn them into different ways to find something worth reading.
In the Field Notes From Everywhere Publication, you'll find editorial lists built around the moods, characters, relationships, tropes, settings and experiences readers are looking for.
And for readers who would prefer considerably more books to choose from, there's The Reading Room.
Every Monday through Saturday, we take a different reading theme and send members a catalogue of at least 30 recommendations. Every issue also joins a growing archive and book catalogue, so there's always somewhere else to look when you need another book.
Of course, all of that depends on the recommendations being worth paying attention to in the first place.
So we don't just collect them.
### 4.3 How we find the books
Every list starts with a lot of digging.
We analyse multiple independent reader discussions about a specific reader interest to gather the books people recommend across all of them.
Depending on the theme, this process can leave us with hundreds to thousands of individual recommendations to work through.
From there, we go through the recommendations one by one. Each is manually reviewed to make sure someone is genuinely recommending the book for what we're researching, and every book is verified (via our internal system) to make sure we have the right title and accurate metadata.
Recommendations for the same book are then brought together so we can see how often each title was recommended across the discussions we analysed.
And that's really what we're looking for.
Not simply books that could fit the theme, but the books readers themselves keep putting forward for it.
Some titles might receive a single recommendation. Others come up again and again. Once all of that evidence is brought together, we can see which books have the strongest reader support and use the top recommendations to build the final list.
So when you see that an FNFE list is based on hundreds of reader recommendations, we don't mean there are hundreds of books on the list. We mean the books you see were selected from a much larger body of reader recommendations that we collected, reviewed and validated for that particular theme.
And particular matters too. We research every theme independently. A book being highly recommended for one theme doesn't automatically qualify it for another — it needs reader recommendation evidence for that one too.
You'll find a How we made this list note near the top of every Publication article with details about the research behind that specific list. If you've followed the link from one of those notes to get here, well, now you know what we mean.
### 4.4 Our recommendations aren't for sale
The books that make our lists get there because of the reader recommendation evidence behind them.
Publishers, authors, advertisers, sponsors and other commercial partners can't pay to change those results or buy a place on one of our recommendation lists.
FNFE may make money through things like subscriptions, advertising, sponsorships or affiliate relationships, but those commercial relationships are kept separate from the research that determines which books we recommend.
You can read more about how we handle commercial relationships on our Disclosures page.
### 4.5 Meet the Founder
Hi, I'm Dami, the founder of Field Notes From Everywhere (and the person writing this about page).
Like most readers I've often found myself scouring internet book communities for recommendations. And after doing this for years (with great success and spectacular failures), I found myself thinking: there has to be a more convenient way to find all the genuinely great book recommendations I know are out there. And not just any good recommendation.
And so, Field Notes From Everywhere was born.
There's no shortage of people talking about books online. The problem is that some of the best recommendations are buried inside conversations you'd have to know to look for in the first place.
Field Notes From Everywhere is my attempt to do something useful with all of that.
Find the recommendations. Do the digging. Bring the best of them together. And hopefully make choosing your next book a lot more fun in the process.

## 5. Design Direction
The page should feel editorial, warm, bookish, conversational, and recognizably FNFE — not corporate, institutional, or SaaS-like.
Structure the page so each section visually and narratively leads into the next. Avoid presenting six equally weighted boxes or cards.
The page should have enough visual variation to sustain a long-form About experience, while preserving a clear reading flow.
The methodology callout is the strongest required structural distinction on the page. Other sections do not need to become boxed modules merely for consistency.
Meet the Founder should feel personal and human rather than like a formal executive biography.
Contact should act as a simple closing section rather than a second full Contact-page experience.
The Publication, The Reading Room, and Disclosures references should be capable of linking to their relevant public destinations.
Do not add new product features, editorial claims, trust badges, statistics, team sections, awards, testimonials, or other content that is not specified here.
## 6. Responsive Requirements
The complete About experience must work across desktop, tablet, and mobile.
Preserve the same section order and narrative flow at every viewport size.
Maintain comfortable long-form reading widths and spacing.
The methodology callout must remain prominent, readable, and directly linkable on all devices.
Claude Design may determine responsive typography, spacing, content width, and visual treatment within the established FNFE design system.

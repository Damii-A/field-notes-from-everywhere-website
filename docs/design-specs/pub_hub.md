# FNFE Publication Hub Experience Spec
Publication Category Hubs — Page Flow
The sections below define the actual top-to-bottom flow of each Publication category hub. A reader entering a category hub moves through these sections in this order.
The hub begins by establishing the category and showing what is newest. It then moves into the wider catalogue through a series of exploratory collections, before ending with every article in the category arranged chronologically.
Unless otherwise specified, this sequence should remain consistent as the catalogue grows.
Section 1 → Category Identity
Section 2 → Latest Article
Section 3 → Recent Articles
Section 4 → Browse Our Collections
Section 5 → Genre
Section 6 → Character
Section 7 → Relationship
Section 8 → Trope

## Section 1
### Category Identity
Heading
The name of the Publication category.
Body
A short description of what readers can expect from the category.
Section Brief
The category name and description sit within a single horizontal row. The category name hugs the left side of the composition and the description hugs the right.
This establishes the identity of the hub without repeating the centered masthead treatment already used by the wider FNFE site header.
## Section 2
### Latest Article
Graphic / Interactive Showcase
The most recently published article in the category receives the most prominent position on the page.
The feature is divided approximately 50/50 between the article image and the article title with relevant article information.
Section Brief
This position is automatically determined by publication date rather than manually curated.
It should function as the lead story of the category hub and provide the first obvious entry point into its content.
## Section 3
### Recent Articles
Graphic / Interactive Showcase
A genuinely horizontally scrollable carousel containing a capped number of the articles published most recently after the lead article. The carousel should never expand indefinitely as the Publication grows. The exact cap can be finalized during design and implementation, but once that limit is reached, older articles leave the Recent Articles carousel and remain discoverable through the collection sections and All Articles below.
Each article is represented primarily by its image and headline, with any additional information kept secondary.
The presentation should make it visually apparent that more articles continue beyond the initial viewport. Readers should be able to swipe, drag or otherwise navigate horizontally as appropriate to the device.
Each article remains a normal independent link and preserves expected browser behaviour, including opening in a new tab.
Section Brief
Sections 2 and 3 provide the chronological orientation at the beginning of the hub. After this section, recency stops being the primary organizing principle.

Section Brief — Sections 5–12
These eight sections form the exploratory portion of the hub.
Each surfaces a limited selection rather than functioning as a complete archive for that grouping. The articles shown should not be chosen randomly simply because they belong to the grouping. Ideally, each showcase should surface the strongest-performing or most popular relevant articles within that grouping, using meaningful readership or engagement data once enough exists. Until there is sufficient data to make that judgement reliably, the displayed articles can be selected manually.
The eight groupings and their positions in the hub form part of the stable page structure. The individual articles surfaced within them can change as the catalogue grows.
Although they perform the same basic function, the designer should not treat this as eight visually identical carousels stacked down the page. The category's hub template can use different presentation and movement treatments across the sequence so that scrolling continues to feel exploratory while remaining part of one coherent editorial system.
## Section 13
### All Articles
Heading
All articles
Graphic / Interactive Showcase
A complete chronological catalogue of articles published within the category, ordered from newest to oldest.
Articles are presented as cards, with an initial manageable number displayed.
CTA
See more
Selecting See more progressively reveals additional articles without taking the reader to a separate archive page.
Section Brief
This is the complete retrieval layer of the hub. The collections above are deliberately selective; this section ensures every article published within the category remains accessible.

## Category-Specific Visual Direction
All three Publication category hubs follow the shared page flow defined above, but they should not feel like the same hub with different colours or typography.
Each category should have its own recognizable visual and motion identity based on the editorial purpose of that column. This identity should also carry through into the corresponding article template, so moving from a hub into an article feels like moving deeper into the same editorial world.
The directions below describe what each category's design should communicate. The examples are illustrative rather than requirements. The designer has freedom to determine exactly how these ideas are expressed.
### The Shortlist
The Shortlist should feel bold, selective and decisive. This is the FNFE column built around the books that rise to the top of the reader recommendations we analyse. Its visual world should reflect that sense of selection, prominence and hierarchy without turning the experience into a leaderboard, data visualization or ranking interface.
Possible expressions could include:
strong differences in scale and visual prominence
assertive typography
elements that appear to rise, surface or come forward
crisp, purposeful transitions
a stronger sense of hierarchy than the other two category hubs
graphic treatments that reinforce the idea of a tightly selected editorial shortlist
The design should remain recognizably editorial and playful rather than analytical or database-like.
### What to Read When
What to Read When should feel expressive, fluid and atmospheric. This column approaches books through the reading experience someone is looking for: a mood, feeling, situation, desire or particular kind of experience. Its visual world can therefore feel less rigid and more responsive to the emotional range of the articles being explored.
Possible expressions could include:
fluid or wandering movement
generous use of whitespace
softer transitions between areas of the page
imagery or typography that occasionally breaks from rigid containers
subtle ambient motion
a less regimented presentation of article collections
The category still needs a consistent identity. It should not completely redesign itself according to the mood of each individual article.
### Book Club Book Picks
Book Club Book Picks should feel social, inviting and connected. The visual identity should draw from the idea of books being brought together for shared reading and conversation, without relying on literal book-club clichés.
Possible expressions could include:
articles presented in pairs or groups
visual relationships between neighbouring pieces
elements that gather, overlap or connect
layouts that create moments of juxtaposition
a warmer, more communal sense of movement through the catalogue
recurring visual details that suggest connection or conversation without becoming literal speech bubbles or diagrams
The result should still feel like an editorial publication, not a community platform or social network.
### Across All Three
These directions should influence the whole category environment, not just decorative details. They can inform things such as composition, scale, imagery, typography, movement, transitions, whitespace and the way article showcases behave.
They should not be interpreted as instructions to use specific colours, fonts, animation techniques or layouts.
The goal is simply that a reader should be able to move between The Shortlist, What to Read When and Book Club Book Picks and feel that they have entered three distinct parts of the same FNFE publication.

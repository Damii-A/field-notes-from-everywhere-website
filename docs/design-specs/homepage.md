# FIELD NOTES FROM EVERYWHEREHomepage Experience Specification
Working document — September 2026
## 1. Homepage Section 1 — Hero / Opening Experience
### Heading
Your TBR list is about to get a lot longer. Consider yourself warned.
### Graphic / Animation
Animated Reader Request Stratosphere: a full-width ambient field of multiple real-reader-style book requests drifting slowly and independently across the lower part of the hero.
### Section Brief
This is the homepage opening experience. It should interrupt passive browsing and immediately make an avid reader feel that FNFE is for them. It introduces FNFE through personality, recognition and abundance rather than explanation. The research methodology is deliberately held back for Section 2.
Directly beneath the headline is the Animated Reader Request Stratosphere: a large ambient field filled with the kinds of things readers say when describing what they want to read next. The mental model is a dump of reader requests or thoughts floating around the reading stratosphere. It should feel like the visitor is looking into a collective stream of thoughts, cravings, moods and oddly specific book requests.
The Stratosphere should quietly demonstrate the breadth of reading interests FNFE covers without explaining Tags. Requests can span genres and subgenres, tropes and relationship dynamics, moods and emotional experiences, character types and dynamics, settings and world elements, reading situations and specific reading desires.
It should not look like a carousel, ticker, testimonial rotator, tag cloud, card grid or conventional UI component.
Illustrative requests only:
"I want a book that will absolutely destroy me."
"What's the scariest book you've ever read?"
"Give me the slowest slow burn imaginable."
"I need something I genuinely won't be able to put down."
"Any good adult coming-of-age books?"
"I want a fantasy with a morally grey heroine."
The final request bank will be developed separately. Requests should sound like things real readers could genuinely ask in a book-recommendation community, rather than Tag names converted into questions.
Animation and layout guidance:
Show multiple reader requests simultaneously, scattered irregularly rather than arranged in rows or columns.
Each request is standalone text. Requests drift slowly and independently, with subtle variation in direction, speed and trajectory.
Requests gradually enter and leave the visible area. Some can be partially cropped at the viewport edges.
There should be no obvious beginning, end or slide transition. Motion must remain slow enough for requests to be comfortably readable.
Subtle variation in size, prominence, opacity, position or perceived depth is acceptable. The overall effect should feel abundant and alive, never frantic or chaotic.
Requests are atmospheric only and are not clickable links, filters, tags or buttons.
The headline sits within the normal content width with generous whitespace. The Stratosphere breaks out across the full viewport width and should not feel boxed in.
Starting proportion: roughly 60% headline zone / 40% Stratosphere. The complete opening can sit around one viewport on a typical desktop, but should not be forced into a rigid 100vh.
Desktop can support a richer field. Mobile should show fewer requests with enough spacing to preserve readability.
Respect reduced-motion preferences by showing a static scattered field rather than removing the concept.

## 3. Homepage Section 3 — Field Notes From Everywhere Publication
### Heading
There's plenty to discover in the Field Notes From Everywhere Publication
### Body
Dark fantasy that actually gets dark. Books to read when you need a really good cry. Slow burns that take the "slow" part very seriously. If readers are talking about it, we have a solemn duty to cover it.
### Body
The Field Notes From Everywhere Publication is where we turn those conversations into editorial recommendation lists.
### Heading
Explore our columns
### Graphic / Interactive Showcase
Three vertically stacked editorial column showcases: The Shortlist, WHAT TO READ WHEN, and BOOK CLUB BOOK PICKS. Each showcase includes the column name, a short description, and a carousel/showcase of linked editorial pieces with image + article title. The three showcases should feel visually distinct rather than like one repeated component.
### Section Brief
This section introduces the Field Notes From Everywhere Publication as one of the ways FNFE turns its underlying reader-recommendation research into a reader-facing experience. It should feel like the visitor is entering a lively digital magazine, not being shown a generic content archive.
Under "Explore our columns," present the three FNFE editorial columns as three distinct rows stacked vertically:
The Shortlist
WHAT TO READ WHEN
BOOK CLUB BOOK PICKS
Each row should contain the column name, a short description of what readers can expect from that column, and a carousel or showcase of actual editorial pieces. Each piece should include an image and article title and should function as its own independent article link, preserving normal right-click, Ctrl-click and Cmd-click behavior.
The three rows can share the same basic information hierarchy, but they should not look like the same carousel repeated three times. Each should have enough visual identity that moving into the next row feels like entering a different corner of the Publication.
The exact visual solution is intentionally left to the designer. Motion, transitions, composition, typography, scale, backgrounds and article arrangement can be used to distinguish the rows, but motion must not interfere with readability or article selection.
On desktop, each row should make several pieces available to browse. On mobile, showcases should remain touch-friendly and naturally swipeable without collapsing the three columns into visually identical components.
Overall desired feeling: this should not feel like a page showing three content categories. It should feel like browsing three different corners of a publication, each with pieces worth opening.
